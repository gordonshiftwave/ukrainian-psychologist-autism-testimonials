Good afternoon, my name is Yuliia Markus, and I am a psychologist at the Rivnovaha (Equilibrium) Psychological Assistance Center in the city of Kyiv, Ukraine.

I want to share our experience using the Shiftwave chair in our work with children and teenagers with autism spectrum disorders.

As for the protocols we use: most often it is the ten-minute program in Ukrainian that is adapted for children.

We use the chair either before the main lesson, to make it easier for the child to get engaged in the work, or after an intense load, for recovery and to reduce tension.

We choose the intensity individually, depending on the child's state and their reaction to the procedure. Lower levels, in particular up to 5 to 10% (unclear in the audio), we use more often specifically to reduce tension, and in some cases an intensity above 70% for activation.

This is not a universal recommendation, but specifically our practical observations.

We work with children of different ages, including preschool children and teenagers. Our experience with teenagers has been especially interesting and useful for us, because it is not always comfortable to offer them movement exercises or other forms of direct bodily stimulation. But this kind of technological equipment is very interesting to them.

Let me give one example. A teenager with autism came to our sessions twice a week for four months, after a school day at a mainstream school. He was already exhausted, and it was hard for him to focus and quickly engage in interaction with the psychologist. After a ten-minute Shiftwave session, we observed reduced motor tension, faster establishment of contact, better uptake of instructions, and longer sustained attention while doing tasks.

At the same time, children's reactions are very individual. Some children accept both the vibration and the audio accompaniment well. Others refuse the headphones, for example, but respond positively to the vibration itself.

In our opinion, the chair can be especially useful for children who seek deep bodily sensations and have difficulty with self-regulation, or who come to sessions after sensory overload. At the same time, for children with high sensitivity to sound or vibration, the procedure may not be suitable, or may require significant adaptation or gradually getting the child used to it.

We can say for certain that you must not force a child to stay in the chair, ignore their nonverbal signals of discomfort, or automatically apply the same settings to everyone. You need to start gradually, watch the reactions, and if needed turn off the audio accompaniment or stop the procedure.

And our main conclusion from using Shiftwave: it is a very powerful auxiliary tool in comprehensive work with children with autism.

To help more children, it is important to study which sensory profile the chair suits best, to systematically record results, and to compare children's reactions under different settings.

And most important: to see first of all the specific child, their specific needs, their comfort, and their readiness to interact.

Thank you, have a good day!
