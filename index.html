Good afternoon. I am Alona Tolmachova, director of the correctional groups at the Rivnovaha (Equilibrium) Psychological Assistance Center.

I want to share our team’s experience using Shiftwave in work with children with autism.

Most often we use a 10-minute program in Ukrainian called “Adapted for Children.”

We run the session either before a lesson or after it. Before a lesson, our goal is to help the child engage in interaction, lower anxiety, and better sense their own body; after a lesson, to support recovery after the load.

We adjust vibration intensity individually. In our practice, levels up to 50% were more often used to reduce tension, and above 70% were in some cases used for activation before task performance.

This is a description of our experience, not universal settings for every child. We work with children in correctional groups from 4.5 to 10 years old.

We more often noticed practical benefit in children with tension, motor agitation, or difficulty transitioning into organized activity. For children with tactile hypersensitivity, it is convenient that the chair provides bodily sensations of direct contact with a specialist.

At the same time, tolerance varies. Some children refuse the headphones but respond well to the vibration. So refusing the audio accompaniment does not yet mean the whole procedure does not suit the child. If the vibration itself causes discomfort, that needs to be taken into account. Many children refuse the eye mask. In that case the child may keep their eyes open or close them, whichever is more comfortable.

For us it is essential not to force the child, not to ignore nonverbal signs of discomfort, and not to transfer successful settings from one child to another.

The main takeaway: it is important to consider sensory characteristics, individual reactions, and the child’s state on that particular day. We use Shiftwave as an auxiliary component of comprehensive work.

To help a greater number of children, we proposed developing recommendations on gradual familiarization with the chair, separate adjustment of sound and vibration, and a simple observation form before and after the session.

It is worth systematically recording settings, children’s reactions, and changes after sessions, summarizing them, analyzing data received from specialists, and also separately studying the reactions of children with different sensory profiles.
